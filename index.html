01The insight most teams miss

A nearest-neighbour coupling Z_i Z_{i+1} needs a CNOT between connected qubits. When the chip lacks that edge, the compiler inserts SWAPs — each costing 3 extra CNOTs — deepening the circuit and multiplying noise.

🔬 Processor A — the chord problem

Coupling (1,3) is a chord that skips qubit 2. Routing the chain 0-1-2-3 double-backs through the hub, forcing a SWAP and a ~3× depth blow-up.

⬡ Processor B — clean cycle

A heavy-hex lattice containing a clean 6-cycle 0-1-2-3-4-5-6. The identical chain maps with zero SWAPs at native cost.

⚙️ Processor C — our design

A 6-qubit open path matching B's best cost with half the qubits, plus a spare qubit for ancilla error mitigation. The co-design payoff.

04Honest methodology

✅ What we measured

Exact diagonalisation gives the ground-truth to beat. VQE runs 4 random restarts with COBYLA. Cost is measured by transpiling onto each real coupling map with optimization_level=3. Noise uses a fair native-circuit comparison at 20k shots.

⚖️ Fairness guarantee

Accuracy columns are identical across processors by construction — we don't claim a physics win that routing can't create. The signal is purely architectural: A pays 50% more CX and 2.8× depth for the exact same answer.
